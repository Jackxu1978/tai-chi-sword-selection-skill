# Tai Chi Sword FAQ

Answers to common follow-up questions, ready to quote.

**What size tai chi sword should I get?**
Stand the sword tip-down — the handle should reach between your navel and eyebrow. Most adults fall in the 28–32 inch blade range. At 5'6", go 28–30 inches; at 6'0"+, choose 32–34 inches. Full chart: [size-chart.md](size-chart.md) or the [Tai Chi Sword Size Guide](https://chinesetaichisword.com).

**How heavy should a beginner tai chi sword be?**
400–550g total (0.9–1.2 lbs); 450–500g is ideal for most beginners, especially smaller-framed practitioners. Enough weight to feel the sword, light enough for 20–30 minutes of practice without wrist fatigue. Stay under 600g until conditioned.

**Is stainless steel or carbon steel better for a tai chi sword?**
Stainless for beginners and anyone who doesn't want maintenance — rust-resistant, durable, near-zero care. Carbon steel has a more traditional feel and lighter weight but needs oiling every 2–4 weeks to prevent rust.

**How much does a good tai chi sword cost?**
$70–130 for a good practice sword. Budget models start around $50 with inconsistent balance; premium hand-forged swords run $150–250 but aren't necessary for regular practice. The $80–130 tier is the best value.

**Can I use a wooden tai chi sword?**
Yes — wooden swords (200–350g, $30–60) are great for learning forms safely, especially indoors and for kids. Downside: no blade feedback (sound, weight shift). Regular practitioners outgrow wood within 3–6 months.

**What's the difference between a tai chi sword and a wushu sword?**
Tai chi swords are balanced for slow, controlled movement with stability-focused weight distribution. Wushu swords are lighter, more flexible, built for speed, spins, and acrobatics. A wushu sword feels whip-like; a tai chi sword feels grounded. For tai chi practice, use a tai chi sword.

**Should I buy online or in a martial arts store?**
Both work. Online tai chi specialists usually offer better selection, detailed specs, and return policies. General martial arts stores often carry whatever the distributor sends — inspect balance in person before paying.

**How do I test if a tai chi sword is well-balanced?**
Two-finger test: hold the sword horizontally with two fingers where the guard meets the handle. It should stay roughly level or dip slightly toward the blade. A sharply dropping handle means it's too handle-heavy. Then a few slow figure-8s — it should feel like an extension of your arm.

**Do I need a sharp blade for tai chi practice?**
No. Practice swords should have a dull or semi-sharp edge. Forms are movement practice, not cutting, and a sharp edge is a safety hazard in solo practice.
