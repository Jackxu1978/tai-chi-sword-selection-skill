# Solo Practice Tai Chi Jian Engineering FAQ (Master Spec v2.0)

**How do I determine my blade length by stature?**
Use the three stature tiers and verify with the inverted ear test: `28 inch (71 cm)` blade for practitioners up to `5 ft 3 in (160 cm and under)`, `30 inch (77 cm)` blade for `5 ft 3.5 in to 5 ft 8 in (161 to 173 cm)`, and `32 inch (82 cm)` blade for `5 ft 8.5 in (174 cm) and taller`.

**What bare sword weight prevents wrist strain in daily solo forms?**
Around `1.30, 1.32, and 1.39 lbs` (`20.8, 21.2, and 22.2 oz` / `approx. 590, 600, and 630 g`) measured bare without the scabbard across the three stature tiers, paired with a point of balance about `2.4, 3.3, and 3.5 inches (approx. 6.0, 8.5, and 9.0 cm)` ahead of the guard.

**Why is heat treated 2Cr13 stainless steel used for solo forms yet excluded from test cutting?**
Solo Tai Chi sword routines involve zero target impact and expose the blade daily to palm sweat and morning dew. Heat treated `2Cr13` martensitic stainless steel (`AISI 420 / SUS420J1` class) with `0.06 inch (1.5 mm)` blunt safety edges delivers clean elastic rebound in the front third and high corrosion resistance, whereas impact cutting requires high carbon spring steels (`9260 / 1095`).

**How does a recessed scabbard throat liner solve seasonal wood movement?**
Solid hardwood contracts in dry winter heating and expands in summer humidity. Embedding a precision **recessed scabbard throat liner** inside the mouth of the unpainted `1,930 lbf` Jichi hardwood scabbard maintains steady retention and whisper quiet, zero scratch draws year round.
