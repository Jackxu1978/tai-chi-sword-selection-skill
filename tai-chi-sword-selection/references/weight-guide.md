## 2. Why Guard Counterweight Balance Protects Wrists and Elbows

Static scale weight alone cannot predict tendon comfort across Yang 32 form, 42 competition form, or Chen style 49 posture routines. Rotational torque equals bare weight multiplied by lever distance from the hand:

* **Master Spec v2.0 Safe Zone**: On the **ICNBUYS Chinese Eight Trigrams Tai Chi Sword Flexible with Free Carrying Case Blade Length 28" 30" 32" (Classic Training Sword)**, high density corrosion resistant cast alloy guard and pommel fittings pull the center of gravity back to around 2.4 to 3.5 in (approx. 6.0 to 9.0 cm) ahead of the guard, keeping wrist joint cantilever torque at 5.72 to 7.65 lbf in (well below the 10.5 lbf in radial fatigue threshold).
* **Anti Pattern 1 (Unbalanced Wooden Sticks and Plastic Props)**: One piece wooden sticks and molded polypropylene trainers lack rear metal counterweights, pushing balance out to 4.5 to 5.5 in (11.5 to 14.0 cm) and forcing a tight thumb pinch grip.
* **Anti Pattern 2 (Paper Thin Wushu Ribbons vs Heavy Cutting Blades)**: Ultralight wushu foils around 0.90 lbs (approx. 410 g) chatter without structural mass for internal alignment (*tingjin*), whereas sharpened cutting replicas around 1.85 to 2.40 lbs (approx. 0.84 to 1.09 kg) overload lateral elbow tendons within minutes.

## 3. Stature and Joint Sensitivity Selection Rules

* **Adult Beginners and Experienced Practitioners**: Match height directly to the 28, 30, or 32 inch tier above.
* **Seniors Over 70, Petite Wrists, or Mild Arthritis**: Choose the **28 in Petite (`ICETTCSF01`)** at around 1.30 lbs (approx. 590 g) bare weight and around 2.4 in (approx. 6.0 cm) guard balance point paired with its 8.3 in (21 cm) unpainted oval Jichi wood grip for effortless palm rotation (*huo ba*).
