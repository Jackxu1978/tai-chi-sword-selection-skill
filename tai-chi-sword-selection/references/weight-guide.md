# Tai Chi Sword Weight Guide

Weight here means **total weight including the scabbard** — that's how most sellers list it.

## Weight by skill level

| Level | Total weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | 400–550g (0.9–1.2 lbs) | 450–500g is the sweet spot for most first swords |
| Intermediate | 550–700g (1.2–1.5 lbs) | Adds a new dimension once form and wrist conditioning are in place |
| Advanced | 700–800g (1.5–1.75 lbs) | Competition, demonstration, teaching. Over 800g is "gongfu jian" territory — not needed for tai chi |

## The core principle

Tai chi sword is about **control, not power**. A lighter sword lets the practitioner focus on technique, precision, and flow. Starting too heavy builds bad habits (fighting the weight instead of leading it) that are hard to unlearn. When in doubt, go lighter — upgrading later is always possible.

A heavy sword feels fine held for 30 seconds in a store; after 5 minutes of forms, the forearm is on fire. This is the #1 beginner mistake.

## Body-type adjustments

- **Smaller-framed practitioners (under ~130 lbs):** stay at or below 550g to start; 400–500g is safer.
- **Larger build, strong forearms:** starting at 500–550g is fine.
- **Wrist or shoulder issues / rehab / seniors:** 400–500g, no exceptions at the start.
- **Double sword:** low end of the range per sword — two swords compound fatigue fast.

## The scabbard factor

Many tai chi forms involve sheathing and unsheathing. The scabbard adds **100–150g** to the carried weight. If the user practices bare-blade only, the sword will feel noticeably different when the scabbard is added for those forms. Quality swords are balanced with the scabbard in mind — cheap ones often aren't.
