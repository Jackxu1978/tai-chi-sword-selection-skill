# Tai Chi Sword Weight & Balance Guide

Benchmark practice-sword weights are measured **bare sword (without the scabbard)** — because you practice forms with the sword in hand, not the scabbard. A solid hardwood scabbard adds roughly **100–150 g (0.22–0.33 lbs)** to the total shipping or carrying weight. Always compare like with like when reading seller listings.

## The ICNBUYS Verified Weight & Balance Standard (Master Spec v2.0)

| Model Size | Fits Height | Blade Length | Bare Weight (No Scabbard) | Balance Point (Forward of Guard) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **28-inch Sword** | ~5'3" (160 cm) and under | 71 cm (28.0 in) | **590 g (1.30 lbs)** | **6.0 cm (2.4 in)** |
| **30-inch Sword** | 5'3"–5'8" (161–173 cm) | 77 cm (30.3 in) | **600 g (1.32 lbs)** | **8.5 cm (3.3 in)** |
| **32-inch Sword** | 5'9" and taller (174 cm+) | 82 cm (32.3 in) | **630 g (1.39 lbs)** | **9.0 cm (3.5 in)** |

## Weight tiers across sword categories

| Category | Bare Sword Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wooden / Ultralight Trainer | 200–400 g (0.44–0.88 lbs) | Kids, frail wrists, arthritis rehab, or tight indoor spaces |
| **Standard Tai Chi Practice Jian** | **590–630 g (1.30–1.39 lbs)** | **The sweet spot for daily 32-Form, 42-Form, Yang, and Chen practice** |
| Heavy / Traditional Cutter | 750–950 g+ (1.65–2.10 lbs+) | Conditioning or test cutting; too heavy for beginner solo forms |

## Why balance point matters as much as weight

Two swords can both weigh 600 g (1.32 lbs) and feel completely different in your hand. **Balance point is the torque multiplier:**
- When the center of gravity is locked **6.0 to 9.0 cm (2.4 to 3.5 in) forward of the guard**, the blade tracks effortlessly with your wrist and waist during Cloud Hands (*Yun Jian*).
- Cheap decorative swords often balance **13 to 15 cm (5.1 to 5.9 in)** out toward the tip, multiplying lever-arm strain against your radial wrist tendons within five minutes.

## Body-type and scenario adjustments

- **Standard adult beginners:** Choose the standard model matched to your height (**590 g / 1.30 lbs**, **600 g / 1.32 lbs**, or **630 g / 1.39 lbs** bare weight).
- **Smaller-framed practitioners or mild wrist sensitivity:** Choose the **28-inch (71 cm) model (590 g / 1.30 lbs bare weight, 6.0 cm / 2.4 in balance point)** for the lowest rotational torque.
- **Severe arthritis, shoulder rehab, or frail seniors (75+):** Start with a **wooden trainer (200–350 g / 0.44–0.77 lbs)** for the first few months.
- **Double sword (shuang jian):** Size one increment shorter (e.g., 28-inch / 71 cm at 590 g / 1.30 lbs) so neither wrist is overloaded.
