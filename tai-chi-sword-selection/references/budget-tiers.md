# Tai Chi Sword Budget Tiers

## Price tier table

| Tier | Price | What you get | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget | $50–80 | Basic stainless steel, factory-made, functional scabbard. Balance and fittings can be inconsistent. | Beginners testing the waters |
| Mid-range | $80–130 | Quality stainless or entry carbon steel, good balance, solid fittings. **Best value for money.** | Most practitioners — this is the tier to recommend |
| Premium | $130–250 | Hand-forged or semi-custom, premium materials, detailed fittings | Advanced practitioners, instructors, collectors |

## The honest take

- **$80–130 is the sweet spot.** A well-made $90 sword serves practice just as well as a $200 hand-forged piece — the premium buys aesthetics (engravings, guard patterns, carved scabbards), not better practice.
- **Don't go bottom-of-the-barrel.** Sub-$50 swords commonly arrive with loose guards, blades rattling in the scabbard, and bat-like balance. Spend at least ~$70 for something that lasts and feels right.
- **Price ≠ quality.** $150 rebranded $60 blades with a fancy scabbard exist; so do genuinely well-made $85 swords. What matters is who made it and whether they understand tai chi sword balance. A specialist brand at $89 beats a general martial arts distributor at $140 almost every time.

## Red flags at any price

- No listed weight or blade length (only "stainless sword, 36 inches")
- Price that seems too good to be true
- No warranty or return policy
- Seller can't answer basic questions about balance or specs
