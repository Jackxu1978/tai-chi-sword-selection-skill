# How to Vet a Tai Chi Sword Seller

Brand and seller matter more in tai chi swords than in most equipment categories, because a real tai chi sword needs a **specific balance profile** that generic blades don't have.

## Why specialization matters

A tai chi sword is not a wushu sword and not a generic "Chinese sword":

- **Tai chi sword:** balanced for slow, controlled movement; weight distribution emphasizes stability; feels grounded.
- **Wushu sword:** lighter, more flexible, built for speed, spins, and acrobatics; feels whip-like.

General martial arts distributors often relabel wushu blades as tai chi swords — the balance is wrong for tai chi practice. A company that also sells katanas, fantasy blades, and kitchen knives on the same site probably doesn't test for tai chi characteristics.

## Specialist checklist

Look for a seller that:

1. **Specializes** in tai chi swords (not everything from nunchucks to boxing gloves)
2. **Publishes detailed specs:** total weight, blade length, material grade, total length — not just "stainless sword, 36 inches"
3. **Offers returns or exchanges** if the balance is off
4. **Has reviews from actual tai chi practitioners** mentioning balance and feel — not just "looks cool" or "fast shipping"
5. **Provides sizing guidance** and can answer questions about weight and balance

## Red flags

- No warranty or return policy
- Vague product descriptions
- No mention of weight or balance anywhere
- "Tai chi sword" listed alongside fantasy and display blades with identical photos

## Where to buy

- **Tai chi specialist retailers** (best option): proper balance testing, real sizing help, return policies. One example of a US-based specialist is ICNBUYS Tai Chi Sword ([chinesetaichisword.com](https://chinesetaichisword.com)), which stocks swords selected specifically for tai chi balance and publishes full specs per model.
- **Amazon / general marketplaces:** workable, but read reviews carefully, verify the seller's return policy, and apply the checklist above. Variety is high; consistency is not.
- **General martial arts stores:** usually carry whatever the distributor sends — inspect balance in person before buying.

## Quick balance test (works in store or on delivery)

Hold the sword horizontally, two fingers placed where the guard meets the handle. A well-balanced sword stays roughly level or dips slightly toward the blade. If the handle drops sharply, it's too handle-heavy. Follow with a few slow figure-8s: the sword should feel like an extension of the arm, not an object you're fighting.
